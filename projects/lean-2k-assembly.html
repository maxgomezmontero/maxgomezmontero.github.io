Lean redesign proposal for a paint-mixing system assembly

Time study, value stream map and 5-Whys on the assembly of a two-component paint mixing & dosing system. Only 15.5 of 666 lead-time hours added value — so we proposed fixing the flow.

Role
Team member · team of 5
Where
Bradley University · with Dove Equipment
When
Spring 2025 (final deck May 5, 2025)
Methods
Time study · VSM · muda costing · 5 Whys · 5S · kanban · poka-yoke
Status
Proposal (not implemented)

Problem

Assembling one mixing system took 15.5 h of work but 666.65 h of lead time. Operators walked for tools, waited for parts, picked the wrong fittings and redid work when customers changed specs.

What I did

Mapped the current value stream, costed five wastes, ran 5-Whys on each, and proposed an assembly cell with 5S, a kanban + poka-yoke loop and a standard procedure, phased over 6 weeks.

Result

Estimated $16,513/year in savings for a $12,390 investment: a 9-month payback. The plan stayed a proposal.

05Key decisions

Decision

Fix flow, not capacity

At 15 units a year takt is 133 h per unit against 15.5 h of processing. The line wasn’t short of capacity; work was waiting.

Decision

Mistake-proof the spec gate

Over-processing came from customers changing specs after parts were ordered. A kanban + checklist forces a customer check-in before purchasing.

Decision

Conservative savings

Savings assume 75% fewer defects and 80% less motion — not perfection.

Decision

Phase it

A 6-week plan: build the cell, then kanban and poka-yoke, then the SOP and training.
