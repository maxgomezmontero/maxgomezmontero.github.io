Predicting surface roughness in wire EDM, while cutting

Surface roughness is usually checked after the part is cut. We predicted it during the cut, from the machine controller’s own signals — published in Neural Computing and Applications (Springer, 2026).

Problem

Models built on fixed machine settings assume a steady process. Real wire-EDM cuts drift, so parts still get measured after machining.

What I did

Turned the controller’s voltage, current and feed-rate readings into 12 online features and trained a neural network on a designed experiment of 81 cuts.

Result

~95.8% average prediction accuracy (published). On the test set the network never accepted an out-of-spec part (0 Type I errors).

Study designMy roleResultsFollow-up work

01Study design

  • Machine & material: Sodick VZ-300L, 0.25 mm brass wire, 2507 duplex steel; roughness measured on a Mitutoyo SJ-301.
  • Experiment: 3³ full factorial — wire tension, supply voltage and feed rate at three levels each, three replicates: 81 cuts, split 64 for training and 17 for testing.
  • Inputs: 12 online features from the controller — maximum, minimum, difference and average of voltage, current and feed rate.
  • Model: 12 inputs → 4 hidden layers × 8 neurons (ReLU) → 1 output; learning rate 0.001, 20,000 epochs, MSE loss.
  • Decision rule: accept the part if predicted Ra ≤ 80 µin.
